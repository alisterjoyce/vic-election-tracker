Glossary

The election terms this site uses, in plain words. Wherever one appears on the site it has a dotted underline: tap it for a short definition and a link back here. For the rules themselves, the Victorian Electoral Commission is the authority.

2PP · 2CP · Primary vote · Swing · Notional margin · Quota · Above the line · Below the line · Preference flow · How-to-vote card · Ballot draw · Early votes · Postal votes · Final two

Two-party preferred (2PP)
The vote after all preferences are counted, as if the contest were only between Labor and the Coalition. Victoria's lower house uses full preferential voting, so every formal vote ends up with one side or the other. Statewide 2PP figures from polls are the usual headline (for example, Labor 48, Coalition 52). In a seat where the last two candidates aren't Labor and Coalition, the 2PP is a calculation, not the real final count: see 2CP.
Two-candidate preferred (2CP)
The vote after preferences between the two candidates left at the end of the count in a seat, whoever they are: Labor and Greens, Liberal and an independent, Nationals and One Nation, and so on. A seat's 2CP decides who wins it. The site uses 2PP for Labor-v-Coalition contests and 2CP for every other final pair.
Primary vote (first preferences)
The number 1 votes a party or candidate gets, before any preferences are counted. Polls report primary votes for each party; the count on election night starts with them. A candidate with more than half the primary vote wins outright; otherwise preferences decide.
Swing
The change in a party's share of the vote since the last election, in percentage points (a swing of +3.0 to the Coalition means its share rose by three points). On the pendulum, a "uniform swing" applies the same 2PP change to every Labor-v-Coalition seat, a simple what-if that real elections never follow exactly.
Notional margin
How far the leading side's 2PP or 2CP vote was above 50% at the last election, recalculated for the seat's current boundaries (and adjusted for any by-election). A margin of 4.6 means a swing of more than 4.6 points against the holder would, on its own, change the result. This site uses the ABC's notional margins.
Quota
In the upper house, each region elects 5 members, and the share of the vote that elects one member is a quota: one-sixth of the formal vote, plus one vote (about 16.7%). A group with more than a quota elects a member and its surplus moves on by its voters' preferences; seats left over go to the groups closest to a quota once preferences are counted.
Above the line
On the upper-house ballot paper, numbering the group boxes above the thick line. From 2026 voters number at least 5 boxes; the vote goes to each group's candidates in that group's own order, then on to the next group you numbered. Group voting tickets, where a party chose your later preferences, no longer exist.
Below the line
On the upper-house ballot paper, numbering individual candidates below the line, at least 5 of them, in your own order, across any groups.
Preference flow
Where a candidate's votes go when that candidate is excluded from the count (or elected with a surplus): each ballot moves to the next candidate its voter numbered. A "flow" is the share going each way, for example 80% of Greens preferences to Labor. Flows decide most lower-house seats and the last upper-house seats.
How-to-vote card
A party's or candidate's suggested way to number the ballot paper, handed out near voting centres. Cards must be registered with the VEC, which publishes them. Voters don't have to follow any card; the site shows them as each party's or candidate's own recommendation, never ours.
Ballot draw
The VEC's random draw, held after nominations close, that sets the order of candidates on each lower-house ballot paper and the order of groups (and ungrouped candidates) on each upper-house paper. For 2026 it was held at 4 pm on Monday 9 November.
Early votes
Votes cast in person at an early voting centre in the days before election day (from Wednesday 18 to Friday 27 November in 2026). Anyone can vote early. Early votes are counted from election night, and the VEC publishes how many have been cast each day.
Postal votes
Votes from people who applied for a postal vote and send their ballot papers back by post (or drop them at a voting centre). They are counted in the days after election day, as long as they arrive by the VEC's deadline, so close seats can take a week or more to settle.
Final two
The two candidates left at the end of a seat's count, after everyone else has been excluded and their preferences passed on. The one with more of the 2CP vote wins. Models such as Australian Election Forecasts publish the chance of each possible final pair.
