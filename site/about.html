About and methodology

An independent tracker for the Victorian state election on Saturday 28 Nov 2026. It isn't affiliated with the Victorian Electoral Commission, the ABC or any party. It collects published facts and links to the sources; it doesn't republish anyone's articles or profiles.

The tracker lives at alisterjoyce.github.io/vic-election-tracker, hosted on GitHub Pages; its code and data are on GitHub.

What each number means

Margin
The seat's notional margin from the ABC election guide: how far the leading party's two-party (or two-candidate) preferred vote was above 50% at the 2022 election, adjusted for by-elections. "ALP 4.6 vs L/NP" means Labor would lose the seat on a uniform swing of more than 4.6 points to the Coalition. Shown to one decimal place.
Classification
The ABC's own label for the seat, such as "Marginal Labor" or "Very Safe Liberal", taken from its guide.
Seat counts (home page)
Sitting members by party going into the election, not a projection. The majority line is 45 of 88 seats in the Assembly and 21 of 40 in the Council.
Poll of polls
A smoothed average of every published statewide poll. Each poll is weighted by the square root of its sample size and by distance in time (a Gaussian kernel with a three-week standard deviation). Dots are individual polls. Independents are counted in "Other" because pollsters report them differently.
Swing-adjusted margin
The current poll-of-polls Coalition two-party-preferred vote minus its 2022 result (45.0%) gives a statewide swing, shown with a sign (for example +9.7 to L/NP). That swing is applied uniformly to the seat's margin: "ALP 4.6 → L/NP 4.4 on current polling". It's only calculated for Labor-versus-Coalition seats. Uniform swing is a rough guide, and One Nation's vote breaks it in outer-suburban and regional seats.
Forecasts
Each seat's outlook lists every model's call side by side, each with its date, so you can see where they disagree: Australian Election Forecasts (aeforecasts.com), a simulation model that gives each candidate a probability of winning, a first-preference range (the model's 90% range, 5th to 95th percentile, around its median) and the likeliest final two-candidate pairings; it's refreshed four times a day; the YouGov MRP (multilevel regression and post-stratification) call, with the projected runner-up and two-candidate share it published; RedBridge's seat model, which so far has published statewide totals only, not seat ratings; and the Sportsbet favourite. A line above them says whether the calls agree. The home page's "Models" bar adds up Australian Election Forecasts' win probabilities into expected seats per party (a 60% chance counts as 0.6 of a seat), with RedBridge's totals beneath it, and the map can be coloured by its predicted winner, shaded by how likely the win is. The models are other people's work: they're shown with credit and links, and the tracker doesn't adjust them.
Betting favourite
The shortest-priced option in Sportsbet's seat market, as decimal odds (1.72 returns $1.72 per $1, stake included), with the date the prices were taken. When two options share the shortest price they're shown as joint favourites. "Any Other" is never counted as the favourite. Odds reflect where money is being bet, not a probability of winning. For information only, not betting advice. Gambler's Help: 1800 858 858.
Seat polls
Published single-electorate polls, with pollster, date, sample and the two-candidate result. Single-seat polls have small samples and wide error margins.
Candidate status
VEC-nominated: on the Victorian Electoral Commission's list after nominations close. Endorsed: preselected or endorsed by the party, or listed in the ABC guide. Reported: reported but not independently confirmed. Announced: listed on Wikipedia and added automatically. Superseded: replaced by a newer ABC entry for the same party and seat. These rows are kept in the data but hidden on the page. Withdrawn: no longer standing.
"N candidates tracked"
The count of active candidates in both houses, after each refresh's check of every name on the ABC's A-Z candidate list. Each refresh also compares the list with electiontracker.au and reports any candidate it lists that the tracker doesn't have, for checking by hand. Hover over the footer line for the lower-house comparison.
Retiring
A sitting MP or MLC who isn't recontesting, from the ABC's guide and its retiring-MPs list. Each refresh also reads electiontracker.au's list of retirements: someone it lists that the tracker doesn't flag is marked retiring when its source is a party or Parliament announcement or a mainstream news outlet, and otherwise reported for checking by hand.
Policies
The Policies page sets out each major party's published position on 15 issues, from Australian Election Tracker's policy ledger, with every claim linked to its manifesto, release, Hansard record or costing. It is a ledger of published claims, not a rating: an empty cell means no sourced position has been recorded yet, not that a party has no view. The Liberal and National parties can be shown combined (where they share a policy) or separately.
The map
Districts coloured by the current holder; by margin band (the ABC's Marginal, Safe and Very Safe, shaded in the leading party's colour); by Sportsbet favourite (dashed outline for joint favourites); and, once counting starts on 28 Nov, by result. The Regions view shades each upper-house region by the party with the most current MLCs. The Melbourne inset shows the small metropolitan seats. Boundaries are simplified to about 60 m for speed.
How it voted in 2022
Each district's final 2022 result from the VEC: first preferences, two-candidate preferred, and the swing since the notional 2018 result on today's boundaries (the 2021 redistribution changed many seats, so raw 2018 results aren't comparable). On the booth map, each voting centre is coloured by its two-candidate-preferred leader and sized by votes. Early, postal, absent and other declaration votes aren't tied to a place, so they appear in the table instead. On election night the same map will show 2026 booths from the VEC results feed.
Pendulum
Every lower-house seat ordered by its 2022 notional margin, Labor and Greens seats on the left and Coalition and other seats on the right. The slider applies the same two-party swing everywhere (a uniform swing): a seat falls when the swing is bigger than its margin. Only Labor-v-Coalition contests move. Seats contested by Labor and the Greens, or by the Coalition and an independent, have a dashed outline and stay with their holder, because a statewide 2PP swing says nothing about them. Real swings are never uniform, so treat the count as a guide.
Scenarios
Each scenario is tested at every swing on the slider. Seats where Sportsbet's outright favourite is One Nation, the Greens or an independent go to that favourite; every other seat follows the swing. The range shown is the set of swings (and the L/NP two-party vote they imply against 45.0 in 2022) where the scenario holds. "Seats that must fall" are the seats that change hands at the point the scenario is first reached. A current poll (the latest from each pollster in the last 90 days) supports a scenario when its L/NP two-party figure is inside that range.
Quota calculator
Each region elects 5 members; a quota is one-sixth of the formal vote plus one (16.7%). Since 2026 there are no group voting tickets and above-the-line voters number at least 5 boxes, so preferences go only where voters send them and some run out (exhaust). The calculator treats each party as one group: whole quotas elect members, then the lowest party is excluded, a set share of its votes exhausts (you choose it) and the rest goes to parties of the same leaning in proportion to their votes. "Others" in a poll is several small parties, so it can't win as a group. The last-seat margin is the gap between the last party elected and the runner-up when the race was decided.
Preference watch
From 10 Nov, parties and candidates register how-to-vote cards with the VEC. The tracker records each registered card at every refresh and links to it; the preference order printed on the card is entered by hand. Two decisions are flagged: where the Liberal card puts Labor and the Greens in the seven seats Labor and the Greens contest, and where One Nation sends its preferences. Voters can number the boxes however they like; cards are only a recommendation.
News
Headlines that mention a district or its sitting MP, from a Bing News search and from publishers' own feeds, newest first. The tracker stores only the headline, outlet, date and link, never the story. Matching is automatic, so the odd unrelated story can slip in. Google News and the Geelong Advertiser aren't used because their sites ask automated tools not to collect from them.
Downloads and reuse
Every table has a "Download CSV" button, and the data page lists each JSON file with its fields. The tracker's own data is CC BY 4.0; data from the VEC, the ABC, Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0) and Vicmap is credited to them, and photos, headlines and how-to-vote cards belong to their owners.
Timeline
Events of the 2022-2026 term and the election timetable, each in one line with a link to its source. Entries known only to the month show the month.
House effect
On each poll's page, the pollster's figures are compared with the average of every other pollster on the same day (the same smoothing as the poll of polls, leaving that pollster out). A gap that persists across its polls is a house effect, a lean from method rather than from voters.
Search by suburb or postcode
Suburbs and postcodes are matched to districts by land area, from official boundary data. Where one straddles more than one district, every district is listed with its share of the area. Area isn't population, so check your exact address with the VEC.

Election-night results

From 6 pm on Saturday 28 November the tracker reads the Victorian Electoral Commission's results pages every 5 minutes: each district's first preferences, its two-candidate-preferred (2CP) count and the figures for every voting centre, and each region's upper-house first preferences. All the figures are the VEC's; the tracker adds three things of its own, each labelled as such.

Called by this site
A seat is called when the leading candidate's 2CP margin, in votes, is larger than the votes still to be counted plus a safety buffer. The trailing candidate couldn't catch up even if every remaining vote went their way. Votes still to count are estimated as the seat's enrolment times its 2022 turnout, minus the votes counted so far; the buffer is the larger of 250 votes and 1% of enrolment, to allow for rechecks and for the chance that the VEC's indicative 2CP count is between the wrong two candidates. This is the tracker's projection, not the VEC's. A seat the VEC has declared is marked "Declared (VEC)".
Leading and undecided
"Leading" means votes are in but the seat isn't called: the leader is the 2CP leader, or the first-preference leader before the 2CP count starts. "Undecided" means no votes have been counted yet.
Swing and seats changing hands
The 2CP swing compares the leader's 2CP share with their party's 2022 share in the same seat, and is shown only when the two parties in the count are the same two as in 2022. A seat is "changing hands" when its leader is from a different party from the one that holds it.
Upper-house quotas
A region elects five members, so a group with one-sixth of the formal vote plus one (a quota) wins a seat on its own. The region pages show each group's quotas filled on first preferences; the rest of the seats are decided by preferences, which the VEC distributes after election night.

Voting-centre circles on the seat maps use the 2022 locations; centres that are new in 2026 are listed in the table but not mapped.

Sources and refresh times

Data refreshes four times a day, at 6 am, 10 am, 4 pm and 8 pm Melbourne time: polls and candidates from Wikipedia, the full ABC candidate check, seat forecasts, news, betting and how-to-vote cards. Changes are published straight away. The footer and the home page show when the data last changed, in Melbourne time.

What the site doesn't know

  • No candidate is confirmed until the VEC publishes the nominations list after noon on 9 Nov 2026. Until then, every name is endorsed, reported or announced, and late independents are likely.
  • Upper-house tickets are incomplete. Labor's tickets weren't final when the data was seeded, and some group orders are reported rather than confirmed.
  • Betting markets may be partial. Some Sportsbet markets fold longer-priced options under "Show All"; those seats are marked "more options not shown". Prices change constantly, and the tracker shows the date they were taken.
  • Models disagree, and polls age. The YouGov MRP fieldwork predates the July 2026 Labor leadership change. No DemosAU Victorian seat-level MRP was found. RedBridge's model is statewide totals only: its six toss-up seats aren't named in the tracker. A forecast's win probability is the model's, not a certainty.
  • Uniform swing is a simplification, especially with One Nation polling around 20%.
  • Suburb search is by area, not address. Use the VEC to confirm the district for a particular address.
  • Some facts are disputed in the sources, for example an MLC's party status. These are flagged in the notes on the relevant page.

Corrections

Spotted something wrong or out of date? Please open an issue on the project's GitHub page with the page, what's wrong and a link to a source. Corrections are made in the data files, so each fix is visible in the project's history.
