About and methodology

An independent tracker for the Victorian state election on Saturday 28 Nov 2026. It isn't affiliated with the Victorian Electoral Commission, the ABC or any party. It collects published facts and links to the sources; it doesn't republish anyone's articles or profiles.

The tracker lives at alisterjoyce.github.io/vic-election-tracker, hosted on GitHub Pages; its code and data are on GitHub.

What each number means

Margin
The seat's notional margin from the ABC election guide: how far the leading party's two-party (or two-candidate) preferred vote was above 50% at the 2022 election, adjusted for by-elections. "ALP 4.6 vs L/NP" means Labor would lose the seat on a uniform swing of more than 4.6 points to the Coalition. Shown to one decimal place. Where The Poll Bludger's guide lists a different margin (another holder, another two-party contest, or a gap of more than 0.1 points), the seat page notes its figure underneath; the Data page lists every such seat.
Classification
The ABC's own label for the seat, such as "Marginal Labor" or "Very Safe Liberal", taken from its guide.
Seat counts (home page)
Sitting members by party going into the election, not a projection. The majority line is 45 of 88 seats in the Assembly and 21 of 40 in the Council.
Seat projection (home page)
For each party, the median seat count across the published seat models the tracker holds, with the lowest and highest model shown as a range, against the 45-seat majority line. Beside it a half-circle of 88 dots (a hemicycle) shows the medians as whole seats: each party keeps the whole part of its median and the seats left go to the largest fractions, so a party's dots can be one fewer than its rounded median when the medians add up to more than 88. The models are listed beneath it with their dates and links: Australian Election Forecasts (its expected seats, the sum of its win probabilities, so a 60% chance counts as 0.6 of a seat), RedBridge's seat model (its published totals). An MRP poll counts by its predicted winner in each seat, but only once a winner is known for all 88. The YouGov figure is its July 2026 MRP (fieldwork June to July, before the Labor leadership change). YouGov's November MRP for News24 and the Herald Sun is newer, but its seats are published on News Corp sites, which don't allow automated reading, so the tracker holds only the six seats reported elsewhere and no seat totals: it can't give a seat count, so the July MRP stays in this median until November totals can be read. A model is added when one publishes seat figures we may use; The Swing Is On states no licence for reuse and electiontracker.au publishes no seat forecast. With three models the median is the middle one, and the medians needn't add up to 88. The models are other people's work, shown as published; the tracker doesn't adjust or weight them. Until 23 Nov 2026 this spot showed a uniform-swing projection from the poll of polls. It was replaced because a uniform two-party swing can't model One Nation or independents (with One Nation polling around 20% it gave One Nation no seats), so that what-if now lives only on the Pendulum page. The share image from the home page's Share button and the weekly "This week" image use the same models view.
Poll of polls
A smoothed average of published statewide polls. It leaves out polls commissioned by a political party, a union or an advocacy group, and polls with a sample under 800 or none published, following the rule electiontracker.au publishes in its poll methodology (rule D, sample size; rule G and hard exclusion 1, commissioner). Who commissioned each poll (media, the pollster itself, a party, a union or an advocacy group) comes from electiontracker.au's poll ledger (CC BY 4.0) where it lists the poll, and otherwise from the client Wikipedia names; a poll whose commissioner isn't stated stays in. Unlike electiontracker.au, this site applies the rule without case-by-case exceptions (it admits one union-commissioned RedBridge poll from July 2026) and keeps no pollster allowlist or rolling window. Left-out polls stay on the polls lists, greyed, with the reason, and show as empty rings on the chart. Each poll is weighted by the square root of its sample size and by distance in time (a Gaussian kernel with a three-week standard deviation). Dots are individual polls. Independents are counted in "Other" because pollsters report them differently. The shaded band around each line, and the range beside the home page's 2PP and this site's row on the Polls page, is a 95% interval for the average: 1.96 standard errors of the weighted average, from each poll's sampling error (set by its sample size) and from how far the polls in the window spread around the average beyond that (pooled with the same spread across the whole series, so a window with only one or two polls still gets a sensible width). It doesn't allow for error that every pollster shares, so the real uncertainty is wider.
Swing-adjusted margin
The current poll-of-polls Coalition two-party-preferred vote minus its 2022 result (45.0%) gives a statewide swing, shown with a sign (for example +9.7 to L/NP). That swing is applied uniformly to the seat's margin: "ALP 4.6 → L/NP 4.4 on current polling". It's only calculated for Labor-versus-Coalition seats. Uniform swing is a rough guide, and One Nation's vote breaks it in outer-suburban and regional seats.
Forecasts
Each seat's outlook lists every model's call side by side, each with its date, so you can see where they disagree: Australian Election Forecasts (aeforecasts.com), a simulation model that gives each candidate a probability of winning, a first-preference range (the model's 90% range, 5th to 95th percentile, around its median) and the likeliest final two-candidate pairings; it's refreshed four times a day; the YouGov MRP (multilevel regression and post-stratification) call, with the projected runner-up and two-candidate share it published (the November 2026 MRP for the six seats reported outside News Corp's sites: Bendigo East, Kew, Melbourne, Mulgrave, Niddrie and Pakenham; the July 2026 MRP for every other seat); RedBridge's seat model, which so far has published statewide totals only, not seat ratings; and the Sportsbet favourite. A line above them says whether the calls agree. The home page's seat projection takes the median of the seat models' totals, and the map can be coloured by Australian Election Forecasts' predicted winner, shaded by how likely the win is. The models are other people's work: they're shown with credit and links, and the tracker doesn't adjust them. The Swing Is On (Lawrence De Pellegrin), an interactive seat simulator, is linked from the Pendulum page but not shown here: it states no licence that allows its figures to be reused. Its author discloses on its About page that he is a Greens member and staffer.
Betting favourite
The shortest-priced option in Sportsbet's seat market, as decimal odds (1.72 returns $1.72 per $1, stake included), with the date the prices were taken. When two options share the shortest price they're shown as joint favourites. "Any Other" is never counted as the favourite. Odds reflect where money is being bet, not a probability of winning. For information only, not betting advice. Gambler's Help: 1800 858 858.
Seat polls
Published single-electorate polls, with pollster, date, sample and the two-candidate result. Single-seat polls have small samples and wide error margins.
Candidate status
VEC-nominated: on the Victorian Electoral Commission's official list (nominations closed at noon on Mon 9 Nov 2026). From then on the VEC's list decides who is standing, and the name is spelt as the VEC prints it. Endorsed: preselected or endorsed by the party, or listed in the ABC guide. Reported: reported but not independently confirmed. Announced: listed on Wikipedia and added automatically. Superseded: replaced by a newer ABC entry for the same party and seat. These rows are kept in the data but hidden on the page. Withdrawn: no longer standing, including anyone the tracker listed who didn't nominate. These are hidden on seat pages too.
"N candidates tracked"
The count of active candidates in both houses, after each refresh's check of every name on the ABC's A-Z candidate list. Each refresh also compares the list with electiontracker.au and reports any candidate it lists that the tracker doesn't have, for checking by hand. Hover over the footer line for the lower-house comparison.
Retiring
A sitting MP or MLC who isn't recontesting, from the ABC's guide and its retiring-MPs list. Each refresh also reads electiontracker.au's list of retirements: someone it lists that the tracker doesn't flag is marked retiring when its source is a party or Parliament announcement or a mainstream news outlet, and otherwise reported for checking by hand.
Policies
The Policies page sets out each major party's published position on 15 issues, from Australian Election Tracker's policy ledger, with every claim linked to its manifesto, release, Hansard record or costing. It is a ledger of published claims, not a rating: an empty cell means no sourced position has been recorded yet, not that a party has no view. The Liberal and National parties can be shown combined (where they share a policy) or separately.
The map
Districts coloured by the current holder; by margin band (the ABC's Marginal, Safe and Very Safe, shaded in the leading party's colour); by Sportsbet favourite (dashed outline for joint favourites); by Australian Election Forecasts' predicted winner; by model disagreement; and, once counting starts on 28 Nov, by result. Model disagreement counts, for each seat, how many of the sources that call seats one by one (the seat models with a winner per seat, which are Australian Election Forecasts and, in the six seats it covers, YouGov's November MRP, and Sportsbet's outright favourite) name a different winner from the most common call, in a neutral colour that's paler where they agree. RedBridge publishes statewide totals only, so it isn't counted. The Regions view shades each upper-house region by the party with the most current MLCs. The Melbourne inset shows the small metropolitan seats. Boundaries are simplified to about 60 m for speed.
How it voted in 2022
Each district's final 2022 result from the VEC: first preferences, two-candidate preferred, and the swing since the notional 2018 result on today's boundaries (the 2021 redistribution changed many seats, so raw 2018 results aren't comparable). On the booth map, each voting centre is coloured by its two-candidate-preferred leader and sized by votes. Early, postal, absent and other declaration votes aren't tied to a place, so they appear in the table instead. On election night the same map will show 2026 booths from the VEC results feed.
Pendulum
Every lower-house seat ordered by its 2022 notional margin, Labor and Greens seats on the left and Coalition and other seats on the right. The slider applies the same two-party swing everywhere (a uniform swing): a seat falls when the swing is bigger than its margin. Only Labor-v-Coalition contests move. Seats contested by Labor and the Greens, or by the Coalition and an independent, have a dashed outline and stay with their holder, because a statewide 2PP swing says nothing about them. Real swings are never uniform, and a two-party swing can't model One Nation or independents, so this is a what-if tool, not a projection: the home page's seat projection uses the published seat models instead.
Key contests
The Key contests page lists every lower-house seat where the sources favour different winners, or where no candidate's median chance of winning reaches 60%, closest first by the leading candidate's median chance. Each seat shows the same comparison as its seat page's Forecasts: a one-line summary of what each source favours, every candidate with each source's figure and its date, and the likeliest final two beside the 2022 final pair. The sources are the seat models that rate each seat (Australian Election Forecasts, YouGov's November MRP where published) and Sportsbet's prices with the bookmaker's margin removed; RedBridge publishes statewide totals only. The page describes the sources and makes no call of its own.
Build your own prediction
The prediction page lists all 88 seats by region. Each starts on the party most of the seat-by-seat sources pick (the same sources as the map's model disagreement; a tie goes to Australian Election Forecasts, and a seat no source rates starts with its holder), so its starting totals can differ from the home page's medians of the models' seat totals. Change any seat and the hemicycle and totals follow. Your changes are kept in your browser only (see Privacy) and can be shared as an image.
Scenarios
Each scenario is tested at every swing on the slider. Seats where Sportsbet's outright favourite is One Nation, the Greens or an independent go to that favourite; every other seat follows the swing. The range shown is the set of swings (and the L/NP two-party vote they imply against 45.0 in 2022) where the scenario holds. "Seats that must fall" are the seats that change hands at the point the scenario is first reached. A current poll (the latest from each pollster in the last 90 days) supports a scenario when its L/NP two-party figure is inside that range. Above the scenarios, "What the seat models say" shows the same seat-range chart as the home page, and each scenario says how many of the published models give its result on their seat totals.
Quota calculator
Each region elects 5 members; a quota is one-sixth of the formal vote plus one (16.7%). Since 2026 there are no group voting tickets and above-the-line voters number at least 5 boxes, so preferences go only where voters send them and some run out (exhaust). The calculator treats each party as one group: whole quotas elect members, then the lowest party is excluded, a set share of its votes exhausts (you choose it) and the rest goes to parties of the same leaning in proportion to their votes. "Others" in a poll is several small parties, so it can't win as a group. The last-seat margin is the gap between the last party elected and the runner-up when the race was decided.
Preference watch
From 10 to 20 Nov, parties, candidates and others register how-to-vote cards with the VEC. At every refresh the tracker reads the VEC's register, links every card, and reads the preference order each card prints for each district and region from the card's PDF: the number in the box beside each candidate's name (or each group's box above the line), kept only when every number reads cleanly and the names sit in ballot-paper order. A card that can't be read that way (for example a scanned image) is marked "unreadable, see card"; nothing is guessed. Each seat and region page shows every card side by side, lists the candidates with no card, and flags where a Labor, Liberal or Nationals card numbers One Nation or the Greens ahead of the other side of government, as a fact about the card. Voters can number the boxes however they like; cards are only a recommendation.
News
Headlines that mention a district, its sitting MP or one of its candidates by full name, newest first, from publishers' own feeds and from Bing News searches. Each outlet is read through its own feed where its site allows automated reading; where it doesn't (News24, the Herald Sun, The Australian and Sky News Australia ask automated tools not to read their sites) or has no feed, a Bing News search limited to that outlet's site brings in its headlines instead (news.com.au's Victorian politics and Inside Parliament stories arrive this way). Stories about the five party leaders (Ben Carroll, Jess Wilson, Danny O'Brien, Ellen Sandell and Warren Pickering) are also searched for by name, since many name no seat: they go in the statewide list and on the leader's own seat page. A story can also be added by hand on request. The tracker stores only the headline, outlet, date and link, never the story. Matching is automatic, so the odd unrelated story can slip in. Google News isn't used, and the Geelong Advertiser's own feed isn't read, because their sites ask automated tools not to collect from them. The Data page lists every news source and whether it worked at the last refresh.
Downloads and reuse
Every table has a "Download CSV" button, and the data page lists each JSON file with its fields. The tracker's own data is CC BY 4.0; data from the VEC, the ABC, Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0) and Vicmap is credited to them, and photos, headlines and how-to-vote cards belong to their owners.
Timeline
Events of the 2022-2026 term and the election timetable, each in one line with a link to its source. Entries known only to the month show the month.
House effect
On each poll's page, the pollster's figures are compared with the average of every other pollster on the same day (the same smoothing as the poll of polls, leaving that pollster out). A gap that persists across its polls is a house effect, a lean from method rather than from voters.
Your ballot
The Your ballot page (a "Your ballot" button on each seat page, and in search results) shows mock-ups of the two ballot papers for a district, from the VEC's published list: the Legislative Assembly paper in the VEC's ballot order, and the Legislative Council paper for its region, with groups above the line and their candidates below. Names and party names are as the VEC prints them. Beside each paper is every how-to-vote card registered with the VEC for that contest, each labelled as the recommendation of the party or candidate who registered it, and the VEC's rules for a formal vote (from its voter guide; its counting rules, from the scrutineer handbook, are noted as such). The real papers look different; the site recommends no candidate or party.
Where to vote
Each seat page lists the district's early voting centres and election-day voting centres from the VEC's venues spreadsheet, including centres it shares with a neighbouring district, with address and wheelchair access, and pins them on the voting-centre map (positions from the VEC's venue locator). Read once a day; check the VEC's own list before you go.
Early and postal votes
The home page charts the running total of early votes and postal votes returned by day, as the VEC publishes them, against 2022 lined up by days to election day. Seat pages show the district's figures as a share of its enrolment at the close of roll, once the VEC publishes figures by district. Until it does, the pages say so; nothing is estimated. On election night each seat's 2026 panel compares the early and postal votes the VEC reported before polling day with those counted so far, to show roughly how many are still to come.
Compare candidates
On each seat page, a table puts the candidates side by side in ballot order: party, occupation, prior roles and stated priorities, from each candidate's researched profile, where every fact has a source. "Nothing published" means we found no source for it, not that there's nothing to know.
This week
The home page's "This week" button makes a briefing image: the poll-of-polls 2PP and primaries, the seat models' projection (each party's median and range, as on the home page), the three seats whose Australian Election Forecasts win probability or Sportsbet price moved most over the last 7 days (a price counted by its implied chance, 100 divided by the price, so both are in percentage points; moves under a point aren't shown), and the next key date. The latest is always at share/this-week.png, redrawn at each refresh.
Other aggregates
Under the home page's poll of polls, one line gives BludgerTrack's, Ace Strategies' and electiontracker.au's latest figures (their 2PP, or their primaries where they publish no 2PP), each with the date its estimate runs to, linked to the Polls page's comparison.
Order of candidates and parties
Wherever the site shows a number for each party or candidate (seat projections and the models' ranges, the hemicycle, the poll average, the scenarios, betting markets, results tallies, the seat page's forecast table, the share images), it lists them by that number, largest first; where two are equal, they keep the site's fixed party order. Lists of candidates that show no projection (each seat's candidates, the ballot paper previews, the Compare candidates table, region tickets and search results) are in the order the Victorian Electoral Commission drew for the ballot paper (in the upper house, groups in letter order, then ungrouped candidates), or alphabetically by surname where there is no ballot order. Where parties appear without a number (map legends, filters, how-to-vote card columns) or as the columns of a table, they keep one fixed order, alphabetical by name with independents and "Other" last, so rows can be compared down the columns. The pendulum is ordered by margin, which is what it shows.
Search by suburb or postcode
Suburbs and postcodes are matched to districts by land area, from official boundary data. Where one straddles more than one district, every district is listed with its share of the area. Area isn't population, so check your exact address with the VEC.

Election-night results

From 6 pm on Saturday 28 November the tracker reads the Victorian Electoral Commission's results pages every 5 minutes: each district's first preferences, its two-candidate-preferred (2CP) count and the figures for every voting centre, and each region's upper-house first preferences. All the figures are the VEC's; the tracker adds three things of its own, each labelled as such.

Called by this site
A seat is called when the leading candidate's 2CP margin, in votes, is larger than the votes still to be counted plus a safety buffer. The trailing candidate couldn't catch up even if every remaining vote went their way. Votes still to count are estimated as the seat's enrolment times its 2022 turnout, minus the votes counted so far; the buffer is the larger of 250 votes and 1% of enrolment, to allow for rechecks and for the chance that the VEC's indicative 2CP count is between the wrong two candidates. This is the tracker's projection, not the VEC's. A seat the VEC has declared is marked "Declared (VEC)".
Leading and undecided
"Leading" means votes are in but the seat isn't called: the leader is the 2CP leader, or the first-preference leader before the 2CP count starts. "Undecided" means no votes have been counted yet.
Swing and seats changing hands
The 2CP swing compares the leader's 2CP share with their party's 2022 share in the same seat, and is shown only when the two parties in the count are the same two as in 2022. A seat is "changing hands" when its leader is from a different party from the one that holds it.
Upper-house quotas
A region elects five members, so a group with one-sixth of the formal vote plus one (a quota) wins a seat on its own. The region pages show each group's quotas filled on first preferences; the rest of the seats are decided by preferences, which the VEC distributes after election night.

Voting-centre circles on the seat maps use the 2022 locations; centres that are new in 2026 are listed in the table but not mapped.

Sources and refresh times

Data refreshes four times a day, at 6 am, 10 am, 4 pm and 8 pm Melbourne time: polls and candidates from Wikipedia, the full ABC candidate check, seat forecasts, news, betting and how-to-vote cards. Changes are published straight away. The footer and the home page show when the data last changed, in Melbourne time.

What the site doesn't know

  • The candidates are the VEC's official list from the close of nominations at noon on 9 Nov 2026, spelt as the VEC prints them. Names the tracker had that aren't on it are kept in the data as withdrawn but not shown.
  • Upper-house tickets are incomplete. Labor's tickets weren't final when the data was seeded, and some group orders are reported rather than confirmed.
  • Betting markets may be partial. Some Sportsbet markets fold longer-priced options under "Show All"; those seats are marked "more options not shown". Prices change constantly, and the tracker shows the date they were taken.
  • Models disagree, and polls age. YouGov's November MRP is held for six seats only: its full projection is on News Corp sites, which don't allow automated reading, and no seat totals were published elsewhere, so the older July MRP (fieldwork before the July 2026 Labor leadership change) still supplies YouGov's seat count and its other 82 seats. No DemosAU Victorian seat-level MRP was found. RedBridge's model is statewide totals only: its six toss-up seats aren't named in the tracker. A forecast's win probability is the model's, not a certainty.
  • Uniform swing is a simplification, especially with One Nation polling around 20%: it's used only on the Pendulum page, as a what-if, and for each seat's swing-adjusted margin.
  • Suburb search is by area, not address. Use the VEC to confirm the district for a particular address.
  • Some facts are disputed in the sources, for example an MLC's party status. These are flagged in the notes on the relevant page.

Corrections

Spotted something wrong or out of date? Please open an issue on the project's GitHub page with the page, what's wrong and a link to a source. Corrections are made in the data files, so each fix is visible in the project's history.
